COMP 345 Intermediate Project (Team VRW) - Tower Defense: analysis, design and tools

Overview

The project is a tower defense game made of three parts that work together, plus a thin native Windows graphical driver:

Part Folder Classes What it does
1. Map Map/ Map, CellType Grid of path and scenery cells with one entry and one exit, and a validity check.
2. Towers Towers/ Tower, BasicTower, SplashTower, SlowTower Towers that are bought, upgraded, sold and shoot critters.
3. Critters Critters/ Critter, CritterGroup, CritterGroupGenerator Waves of critters that walk the path, die for a reward or leak through the exit, steal coins and cost a life.
4. Graphical driver Gui/ WinGui.cpp Win32 editor and game view that draws and forwards input without changing the rules.
Observers Observer.h, Towers/, Map/, Critters/ Observer, Observable, TowerObserver, MapObserver, CritterObserver, CritterGroupObserver Observer pattern: Tower, Map, Critter and CritterGroup are observable, and each view is re-displayed every time its subject changes (see Observer pattern (Tower, Map and Critter observers)).

Factory: wave creation. Decorator: tower upgrades added during play (see Strategy pattern: tower targeting (Assignment 3, Part 1), Factory pattern: critter waves (Assignment 3, Part 2), Decorator pattern: tower upgrades (Assignment 3, Part 3)). | Every part has its own driver and test program, and GameDriver.cpp plays several waves with the three parts together. Analysis: game rules

Where the rules come from

  • Bloons TD gives the overall rules: the enemies follow a fixed track from an entry to an exit, towers are placed beside the track, every round is harder than the previous one, the player earns cash for every enemy popped and loses lives when an enemy leaks through the exit, and the enemies of later rounds come in a tighter stream. See the Bloons Wiki pages Rounds and Lives. Here the player has 10 lives and loses one for every critter that leaks; the game ends at 0 lives, as the project description says ("the game ends when a certain number of critters are able to reach the end point").
  • League of Legends gives the critters: the lane minions (melee, caster, cannon and super minion) that leave their base one after the other in waves, with stats and gold bounty that grow as the game goes on. See League of Legends Wiki: Minion.
  • The rest comes straight from the project description.

Rules implemented

Map

  1. The map is a grid of any size chosen when it is created. A cell is either PATH or SCENERY.
  2. Critters walk only on PATH cells, towers stand only on SCENERY cells.
  3. There is exactly one entry cell and one exit cell, both PATH cells and different from each other.
  4. The entry and the exit are connected by PATH cells (moving up, down, left or right). Otherwise the map is invalid.
  5. A map can be saved to a text file and loaded again (saveToFile, loadFromFile), so the player can choose a saved map to play on.

Towers

  1. A tower has a buying cost, a refund value, a range, a power (damage) and a rate of fire.
  2. There are three types that differ in what a shot does: direct damage, area damage and damage plus slowing.
  3. A tower has levels (1 to 3). Each level is more powerful and has to be paid for. A tower can be sold at any time for a refund that depends on the coins invested in it.
  4. A working tower detects the critters in its range, selects one and shoots it.

Critters

  1. A critter has a reward, hit points, strength, speed and level.
  2. A critter group is made at the start of every wave, every wave is harder than the previous one.
  3. The critters of a wave enter the map one after the other through the entry.
  4. A critter knows where the exit is, decides where to move and moves at its speed.
  5. Every cell holds only one game element: two critters are never on the same path cell (see Movement in the design).
  6. A critter that reaches zero hit points dies and the player gets coins proportional to its level.
  7. A critter that reaches the exit steals coins from the player according to its strength.
  8. A critter that reaches the exit also costs the player one life. The player starts with 10 lives and the game is over (GAME OVER screen, then restart or go back to the editor) when no lives are left.

Assignment 3 (design patterns)

  1. Targeting. A tower first keeps the critters that are alive and inside its range, then chooses one of them with its targeting option. The four options are the ones of Bloons TD (see Blooncyclopedia: Targeting option): Close* = nearest critter to the tower (the default), First = nearest critter to the exit (the one that walked the most path cells), Strong = most hit points left, Weak = fewest hit points left. On a tie the first critter found is kept. The option can be changed at any time during play.
  2. Waves. Every wave is made by a wave factory from the wave number N (waves destroyed so far + 1): 3 melee and 3 caster critters, N/3 cannon critters and N/10 super critters, all of level N, released every 0.8 s x max(1 - 0.04 (N - 1), 0.4). Later waves are larger, stronger and denser, as in the Bloons TD rounds and the League of Legends minion waves cited above.
  3. Upgrades during play. A tower is upgraded by adding upgrades to it while the game runs, and they can be combined:
    • Level up: the normal way to go up a level (1 to 3). It costs cost x current level (100 then 200 for a basic tower), gives damage and range x1.2 (two level-ups give x1.44) and fire rate x1.1, and half of its price is added to the refund.
    • Splash: the critters within 1.5 cells of the target also take half of the tower's damage.
    • Burning: the target keeps taking 20% of the hit 3 more times, once per second. A new hit restarts the burn, a critter never burns twice at the same time.
    • Freezing: the target is slowed (half speed for 2 s by default). A new hit restarts the slow, it does not add up. Splash (150 coins), Burning (125) and Freezing (100) can each be bought once per tower, at any level. Their price is not refunded when the tower is sold. In the GUI the player buys every upgrade from the selected-tower panel. In the integrated console game (GameDriver.cpp) the towers level up between waves, and a tower at its maximum level buys one special upgrade: the basic tower gets Burning, the splash tower Freezing, the slow tower Splash.

Design

Structure

   Map  <----------- reads ------------  Critter  <---- owned by ---- CritterGroup <---- creates ---- CritterGroupGenerator
    ^                                       ^                              |
    | isSceneryCell (placement rule)        | getRow, getColumn, isAlive   | getActiveCritters()
    |                                       | takeDamage, applySlow        v
  GameDriver  ------------------------>  Tower (BasicTower, SplashTower, SlowTower)  <--- update(deltaTime, critters)

The three parts only know each other through small interfaces: towers use five functions of Critter, critters use five read-only functions of Map (getRows, getColumns, getEntry, getExit, isPathCell) and neither of them knows about the game loop. Coins are always passed as int& (Tower::purchase, upgrade, sell and CritterGroup::update), so the player's balance lives in one variable owned by the game.

One game tick

The game loop (see playWave in GameDriver.cpp) repeats these steps with a small time step (0.1 s):

  1. every tower gets update(deltaTime, group.getActiveCritters()): cooldown goes down, a target is selected and shot if possible;
  2. group.update(deltaTime, map, coins): spawns the critters whose time has come, moves every critter, adds the reward of the critters killed in step 1 and subtracts the coins stolen by the critters that reached the exit;
  3. the wave is over when group.isFinished() is true. Between waves the player buys, upgrades or sells towers.

Towers shoot first so a critter that is killed is paid in the same tick and is never moved afterwards.

Part 1: Map

  • The grid is a vector<vector<CellType>> that starts as all SCENERY, so a blank map of any size is created with Map(rows, columns) (a size that is not positive throws std::invalid_argument).
  • setCell changes a cell, setEntry and setExit store the coordinates and turn that cell into PATH. All of them return false for coordinates outside the map. The entry and the exit cannot be the same cell and cannot be turned into SCENERY.
  • isValidMap checks that entry and exit are set, inside the map, different, PATH cells, and connected.
  • hasConnectedPath is a breadth-first search from the entry over PATH cells with four-direction moves.
  • Read-only accessors (getRows, getColumns, getEntry, getExit, isPathCell, isSceneryCell) let the other parts use the map without being able to change it. isSceneryCell is what a game uses to refuse a tower placed on the path.
  • Choice: the map does not store critters or Tower objects. For the map observer it keeps one letter per cell for the towers standing on it (placeTower, moveTower, removeTower), so a tower can be shown and moved on the map without the Map knowing the Tower class. A tower only stands on free SCENERY, and a cell with a tower cannot become path, entry or exit.

Part 2: Towers

Tower is an abstract base class. The shared behavior (buying, upgrading, selling, range, cooldown, target selection) lives in the base class and each type only provides its own applyAttack and getName. This is the template method pattern: Tower::update is always the same and calls the virtual applyAttack for the part that changes.

Type Cost Damage Range Shots/s Special
BasicTower 100 20 3.0 1.0 direct damage to one critter
SplashTower 150 30 2.0 0.5 damages every critter within 1.5 cells of the target
SlowTower 125 5 3.0 1.0 small damage and halves the target's speed for 2 s
  • Levels: maximum level 3. Going up one level costs cost x current level. In Assignment 1 this was Tower::upgrade (+25% damage rounded up, +0.5 range, +10% fire rate); it is kept for the Assignment 1 and observer tests and drivers, but the game and the GUI now level towers up with the Decorator pattern (see Decorator pattern: tower upgrades (Assignment 3, Part 3)).
  • Refund: half of all the coins invested (purchase plus upgrades), available at any time with getRefundValue.
  • Targeting: among the alive critters inside the range, the one closest to the tower is selected.
  • Cooldown: after a shot the tower waits 1 / fireRate seconds. A tower that is not bought, or already sold, never shoots.
  • Choice: the target is chosen by distance because it is simple to explain and test. More rules (first, strongest) would fit in selectTarget.

Part 3: Critters

Three small classes with one job each.

Class Responsibility
Critter One critter: stats, shortest path, movement, damage, slow, and one-time claimReward / claimPenalty.
CritterGroup One wave: releases the critters one after the other, updates them, settles the coins.
CritterGroupGenerator Called at the start of a wave, generate(wave, map) builds the group. Refuses wave numbers below 1 and invalid maps.

Critter types** (stats at level 1, taken from the idea of the lane minions of League of Legends):

Type Hit points Strength Speed (cells/s) Reward Critters in a wave
Melee 30 1 1.0 2 3 in every wave
Caster 20 2 1.1 3 3 in every wave
Cannon 60 3 0.8 5 wave / 3 (first one in wave 3)
Super 120 5 0.9 10 wave / 10 (first one in wave 10)

The numbers are our own choices. In the game the cannon minion only appears in some waves, here the extra critters stay in the following waves so that no wave is easier than the one before.

Difficulty:** the level of every critter is the wave number, and from level 1 to level n:

  • hit points grow 20% of the base value per level;
  • strength grows by 1 every 3 levels;
  • speed grows 3% per level, at most +50%;
  • the reward is base reward x level (proportional to the level, as the project description asks);

  • the time between two critters is 0.8 s in wave 1 and 4% shorter every wave (never below 0.32 s), like the tighter streams of later Bloons TD rounds. Because stats only go up and a wave never loses critters, the total hit points of a wave strictly increase (checked for waves 1 to 30).

    Movement:** when a critter spawns it runs a breadth-first search from the entry to the exit (it knows the exit from the map) and keeps the shortest path. Each update adds speed x deltaTime to its progress and moves it to the next cell of the path every time the progress reaches 1, so the speed does not depend on the size of the time step. A critter that reaches the last cell escapes.

    One critter per cell:** the project description says a cell holds only one game element. CritterGroup::update gives every critter a function that tells whether a cell is taken by another active critter. Before a critter steps onto its next cell it asks that function; if the cell is taken it stays at 0.8 of its current cell (right behind the critter in front) and tries again on the next update. Critters never pass each other, so the critters are updated front first: the one in front moves and frees its cell for the one behind. A new critter is released only when the entry is free, so a critter that is due can be delayed a few ticks behind a slower one. The exit is never blocked, because a critter that reaches it leaves the map. A single critter used without a group has no neighbours and walks freely.

    Damage, reward and penalty:**

  • takeDamage lowers the hit points, never below zero, and kills the critter at zero. Only a critter that is on the map can be hit.
  • claimReward returns the reward of a dead critter once, claimPenalty returns the strength of an escaped critter once. CritterGroup::update calls them every tick, so rewards and penalties can never be counted twice.
  • A critter that escapes steals as many coins as its strength, and the player never goes below 0 coins.
  • A slow (applySlow) multiplies the speed by a factor for some seconds. A stronger slow replaces a weaker one.

Observer pattern (Tower, Map and Critter observers)

          Observer (interface)                      Observable (subject)
      + update(Observable*) = 0          1   *   - observers : vector<Observer*>
      + subjectDestroyed(Observable*)  <-------- + attach(Observer*) / detach(Observer*)
                 ^                                # notify()   -> calls update(this) on every observer
                 |                                           ^
   +-------------+--------------+------------------+         |
   |             |              |                  |         +----- Tower      (Part 1)
TowerObserver MapObserver CritterObserver CritterGroupObserver +--- Map        (Part 2)
   |  observes   |  observes    |  observes        | observes  +--- Critter    (Part 3)
   v             v              v                  v           +--- CritterGroup (Part 3, add / remove)
 Tower          Map           Critter          CritterGroup
  • Observer.h holds the two base classes (header only, so every existing build command still works). Observable keeps the list of observers; notify() is protected, so only the observed class itself decides when it has changed.
  • Pull model: update(subject) only says "I changed"; the view reads the new values from the subject through its getters. The subjects never know how they are displayed, and the views never change the subjects.
  • Notify only on real changes. A refused action (upgrade past level 3, tower moved onto the path, removing a critter that does not exist, hitting a dead critter) and an action that changes nothing (a cell set to the type it already has) do not notify. A critter notifies when it enters a new cell, not on every small step inside a cell, so the view is not redrawn every tick.
Subject Changes that call notify() View
Tower purchase, upgrade, sell, moveTo, a shot in update (cooldown reset) TowerObserver: type, cell, status, level, damage, range, fire rate, cooldown, costs, refund, splash radius or slow
Map setCell, setEntry, setExit, placeTower, moveTower, removeTower, loadFromFile (once) MapObserver: the grid with coordinates, towers, number of towers and whether the map is valid
Critter spawn, entering a new cell, slow wearing off, escaping (all in update), takeDamage, applySlow CritterObserver: type, level, state, cell and step on the path, hit point bar, speed (and slow), strength, reward
CritterGroup addCritter, removeCritter, an update in which a critter spawns, changes cell, is hit, dies or escapes CritterGroupObserver: one line per critter of the wave, total hit points, spawned, killed, escaped, coins

Lifetime and copies** (the classes are copied by value in the existing code: CritterGroup holds a vector<Critter>, the GUI assigns state.map = Map(...), loadFromFile does *this = loaded):

  • a copy of an observable starts with no observers, because an observer watches one object, not its values;
  • an assignment keeps the object's own observers, so a loaded map is still displayed by the same view (loadFromFile then notifies once);
  • each view attaches itself in its constructor and detaches in its destructor; if the subject is destroyed first, it calls subjectDestroyed and the view forgets the pointer, so there is never a dangling pointer;

  • notify iterates over a copy of the list, so an observer may detach itself while it is being notified.

    Part 3 (add / remove):** the project's Critter is one critter, and the critters of a wave live in CritterGroup. So both are observable: CritterObserver shows one critter changing, and CritterGroupObserver shows critters being added to and removed from the wave (addCritter adds a WAITING critter at the end of the wave, removeCritter(index) also works on a critter that already spawned). As with any vector, adding or removing critters invalidates the pointers returned by getActiveCritters.

    Display:** the views draw text in the console (or into any std::ostream, which the tests use to read what was drawn). The assignment lets us choose how the view is displayed; text keeps the drivers portable and testable. Because the views only use the observer interface, a graphical view (for example a Win32 panel in Gui/WinGui.cpp) can be attached the same way without changing Tower, Map or Critter.

    Drivers** (make observers):

  • tower_observer_driver: creates a Slow tower, plugs a TowerObserver, then buys, upgrades twice, tries a refused upgrade, moves, shoots a critter and sells it. Every view after the first one is drawn by the observer pattern.
  • map_observer_driver: builds a 7 x 7 map, plugs a MapObserver, then places two towers, moves them (one refused move onto the path), adds a shortcut, moves the exit (the view shows the map becoming invalid), reconnects it and removes a tower.
  • critter_observer_driver: plugs a CritterObserver to a critter that spawns, walks, is hit, slowed and killed; then plugs a CritterGroupObserver to the wave 2 group and adds and removes critters (including a refused removal and a critter already on the map).

Strategy pattern: tower targeting (Assignment 3, Part 1)

Role Class
Strategy interface TargetingStrategy (one pure virtual method: selectTarget(candidates))
Concrete strategies NearestToTowerStrategy (default), NearestToExitStrategy, StrongestCritterStrategy, WeakestCritterStrategy
Context Tower, which owns a std::unique_ptr<TargetingStrategy>
  • Tower::selectTarget only builds the list of candidates (non-null, alive, in range) and hands it to the strategy, so the choice of target exists in exactly one place: the strategy. The old hard-coded "closest critter" loop was moved into NearestToTowerStrategy, so the default behaviour of every tower is unchanged.
  • Tower::setTargetingStrategy swaps the strategy at run time and calls notify() (the Observer views refresh). Passing nullptr restores the default.
  • The strategies only see critters, never the tower's internals. NearestToTowerStrategy keeps a pointer to its tower and reads getRow()/getColumn() on every call, so it follows the tower when it is moved.
  • The GUI preview of the next shot (predictTarget) now asks tower.selectTarget, so it always matches the tower's strategy.

Factory pattern: critter waves (Assignment 3, Part 2)

Role Class
Creator interface CritterFactory (createGroup(waveNumber, map), returns a CritterGroup)
Concrete creator StandardCritterFactory
Product CritterGroup (the critters of one wave)
  • The game (GameDriver.cpp, Gui/WinGui.cpp) only asks a CritterFactory for wave N. How many critters of which type and level, and how fast they are released, is the factory's business. Another difficulty curve (an easy mode, an endless mode) would be a new subclass, without touching the game loop.
  • StandardCritterFactory holds a CritterGroupGenerator and delegates to it, so the composition rules written for Assignment 1 stay in one place; the factory adds the common interface. critter_factory_tests checks that both produce identical waves for waves 1 to 30.
  • Wave numbers below 1 and invalid maps are refused with std::invalid_argument.

Decorator pattern: tower upgrades (Assignment 3, Part 3)

Role Class
Component Tower (public operations made virtual)
Concrete components BasicTower, SplashTower, SlowTower
Decorator TowerDecorator: is a Tower, owns the wrapped Tower* and forwards every call to it
Concrete decorators LevelUpDecorator, SplashDecorator, BurningDecorator, FreezingDecorator
  • A decorated tower is still a Tower, so it goes in the same std::vector<std::unique_ptr<Tower>> as the others and the game code does not change: tower.reset(new BurningDecorator(tower.release()));. Decorators stack in any order and number.
  • How a shot goes through the chain. The innermost tower keeps the state (cooldown, purchased, level). When the outermost decorator is updated, it calls the protected performUpdate(deltaTime, critters, attacker) down the chain with itself as the attacker. The innermost tower checks its cooldown, selects the target (with its strategy, but using the attacker's range, so a Level up also widens the targeting) and calls attacker.applyAttack(...). Each decorator's applyAttack first runs the wrapped attack, then adds its own effect. TowerDecorator is a friend of Tower only so it can forward these protected calls.
  • Level up is the upgrade. Each level bought wraps the tower in one more LevelUpDecorator, which overrides: getLevel (wrapped level + 1, so the "Lv2" in the name and the level always agree), getDamage/getRange (x1.2), getFireRate (x1.1, and the cooldown uses the attacker's fire rate so it really shoots faster) and getRefundValue (wrapped refund + half of the price paid, which the decorator stores). On each hit it adds the extra damage of its level to the target. TowerDecorator::getUpgradeCost computes the next price from the decorated level (getCost() x getLevel(), 0 at the maximum level), and TowerDecorator::sell pays the decorated refund. LevelUpDecorator::upgrade always returns false, so the Assignment 1 Tower::upgrade can never run under it and count a level twice.
  • Buying upgrades. TowerUpgrades.h holds the helpers used by the game and the GUI: levelUp(tower, coins) checks that the tower is bought, not sold, below its maximum level and affordable, takes the coins, then does tower.reset(new LevelUpDecorator(tower.release(), cost)). addSplash, addBurning and addFreezing do the same with a fixed price, once per tower. baseTower and findDecorator<D> walk the chain (the GUI uses them for the tower's color and effects).
  • The targeting strategy lives in the innermost tower, and getTargetingStrategy is forwarded, so a strategy chosen before an upgrade is kept after it.
  • BurningDecorator keeps one burn per critter (damage per tick, ticks left, time since the last tick) and advances the burns every time the chain is updated, for the critters in the list passed to the update only, so a critter that left the game (dead, escaped, or a finished wave) is never touched through a stale pointer.
  • Names describe the chain: Basic Tower Lv2 +Splash +Freezing +Burning.
  • Mutating calls on a decorator (purchase, upgrade, sell, moveTo, update, setTargetingStrategy) call notify() on the decorator, so observers attached to it stay up to date.
  • Ownership: the decorator deletes the tower it wraps, so deleting the outermost object frees the whole chain (checked in tower_decorator_tests, and the test programs run clean under AddressSanitizer).
  • Towers are no longer copyable (they own their strategy); the game already stored them in std::unique_ptr.

Other design choices

  • No inheritance for critters: the types only differ in numbers, so one class with a CritterType is enough.
  • Time is passed in seconds to every update, so a console loop, or a GUI later, decides how often things are updated.
  • Critters keep their own copy of the path instead of a pointer to the map, so they cannot be left pointing at a deleted map.
  • Tests and drivers use only the public interface of each class.

Graphical driver

The Win32 graphical driver is a thin layer on top of the same rules used by the console drivers. It does not change the game logic.

  • Edit mode: choose Path, Scenery, Entry or Exit, then click or drag on the grid. Rows and columns are chosen separately and can be resized between 3 and 40, so rectangular maps such as 5 x 30 or 10 x 10 are possible. The "Size step" button makes Rows and Cols change by 1, 5 or 10 at a time. Resizing keeps the path cells, the entry and the exit that still fit. The cell size is computed from the map size (the biggest square cell that fits the map area), and the labels, tower letters and level dots are only drawn when they fit in the cell. Blank map and Sample map are shortcuts for the current size or for the 7x7 map used in GameDriver.cpp.
  • Play mode: buy towers on scenery cells, select a tower to see its stats and buttons, start waves, pause or resume the fixed-step simulation, and switch back to the editor when no wave is running.
  • Selected tower (Assignment 3): clicking a tower replaces the shop with its controls (click an empty cell to get the shop back):
    • Level up (price, or MAX): Decorator pattern, calls levelUp.
    • Target: Nearest / Exit / Strongest / Weakest: Strategy pattern, each click switches to the next strategy with setTargetingStrategy. The current strategy is read back with getTargetingStrategy, and the shot preview follows it.
    • +Splash, +Burning, +Freezing (price, or Owned): Decorator pattern, disabled when already bought or too expensive.
    • Sell (refund). The stats panel shows the decorated name (for example "Basic Tower Lv2 +Burning"), level, damage, range, fire rate, targeting, refund and the effect of each special upgrade. Burning critters get an orange ring, slowed critters a blue one.
  • Resizing: the interface is laid out on a fixed 1100 x 720 design area. The window can be resized freely (down to 640 x 440): the whole design area is scaled by one factor (the smaller of width / 1100 and height / 720) with a GDI world transform and centered, so the map, panel, buttons and texts grow and shrink together and nothing is stretched or cut off. Mouse positions are converted back to design coordinates (toDesignPoint), so clicks, hover and hit tests do not depend on the window size.
  • Simulation: time is advanced with a fixed 0.05 s step and an accumulator. Towers shoot first, then critters move and settle rewards and penalties, so the GUI stays consistent with the console driver.

Known limitations

  • The observer views are console (text) views. The Win32 graphical driver does not use them yet.
  • The graphical interface is only a thin layer. The only additions to the game classes are the read-only accessors needed for drawing: Critter::getExactPosition, Tower::getUpgradeCost, Tower::getMaxLevel, Tower::getTargetingStrategy and TargetingStrategy::getName.
  • Observers attached to a tower stay on the object they were attached to: after an upgrade wraps the tower, they are not moved to the new outer decorator (the GUI and game_driver do not attach observers).
  • Critters follow one fixed path, there is no reaction to towers.
  • Bloons TD features such as layered bloons or end-of-round cash are not used.
  • Game balance (costs, damage, rewards) has not been tuned beyond making the waves get harder.
  • The extra damage of a Level up is added to the main target only, even when the wrapped tower is a splash tower.

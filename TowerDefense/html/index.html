COMP 345 Intermediate Project (Team VRW) - Tower Defense: analysis, design and tools

Overview

The project is a tower defense game made of three parts that work together, plus a thin native Windows graphical driver:

Part Folder Classes What it does
1. Map Map/ Map, CellType Grid of path and scenery cells with one entry and one exit, and a validity check.
2. Towers Towers/ Tower, BasicTower, SplashTower, SlowTower Towers that are bought, upgraded, sold and shoot critters.
3. Critters Critters/ Critter, CritterGroup, CritterGroupGenerator Waves of critters that walk the path, die for a reward or leak through the exit, steal coins and cost a life.
4. Graphical driver Gui/ WinGui.cpp Win32 editor and game view that draws and forwards input without changing the rules.

Every part has its own driver and test program, and GameDriver.cpp plays several waves with the three parts together. Build and run instructions are in README.md.

Analysis: game rules

Where the rules come from

  • Bloons TD gives the overall rules: the enemies follow a fixed track from an entry to an exit, towers are placed beside the track, every round is harder than the previous one, the player earns cash for every enemy popped and loses lives when an enemy leaks through the exit, and the enemies of later rounds come in a tighter stream. See the Bloons Wiki pages Rounds and Lives. Here the player has 10 lives and loses one for every critter that leaks; the game ends at 0 lives, as the project description says ("the game ends when a certain number of critters are able to reach the end point").
  • League of Legends gives the critters: the lane minions (melee, caster, cannon and super minion) that leave their base one after the other in waves, with stats and gold bounty that grow as the game goes on. See League of Legends Wiki: Minion.
  • The rest comes straight from the project description.

Rules implemented

Map

  1. The map is a grid of any size chosen when it is created. A cell is either PATH or SCENERY.
  2. Critters walk only on PATH cells, towers stand only on SCENERY cells.
  3. There is exactly one entry cell and one exit cell, both PATH cells and different from each other.
  4. The entry and the exit are connected by PATH cells (moving up, down, left or right). Otherwise the map is invalid.
  5. A map can be saved to a text file and loaded again (saveToFile, loadFromFile), so the player can choose a saved map to play on.

Towers

  1. A tower has a buying cost, a refund value, a range, a power (damage) and a rate of fire.
  2. There are three types that differ in what a shot does: direct damage, area damage and damage plus slowing.
  3. A tower has levels (1 to 3). Each level is more powerful and has to be paid for. A tower can be sold at any time for a refund that depends on the coins invested in it.
  4. A working tower detects the critters in its range, selects one and shoots it.

Critters

  1. A critter has a reward, hit points, strength, speed and level.
  2. A critter group is made at the start of every wave, every wave is harder than the previous one.
  3. The critters of a wave enter the map one after the other through the entry.
  4. A critter knows where the exit is, decides where to move and moves at its speed.
  5. Every cell holds only one game element: two critters are never on the same path cell (see Movement in the design).
  6. A critter that reaches zero hit points dies and the player gets coins proportional to its level.
  7. A critter that reaches the exit steals coins from the player according to its strength.
  8. A critter that reaches the exit also costs the player one life. The player starts with 10 lives and the game is over (GAME OVER screen, then restart or go back to the editor) when no lives are left.

Design

Structure

   Map  <----------- reads ------------  Critter  <---- owned by ---- CritterGroup <---- creates ---- CritterGroupGenerator
    ^                                       ^                              |
    | isSceneryCell (placement rule)        | getRow, getColumn, isAlive   | getActiveCritters()
    |                                       | takeDamage, applySlow        v
  GameDriver  ------------------------>  Tower (BasicTower, SplashTower, SlowTower)  <--- update(deltaTime, critters)

The three parts only know each other through small interfaces: towers use five functions of Critter, critters use five read-only functions of Map (getRows, getColumns, getEntry, getExit, isPathCell) and neither of them knows about the game loop. Coins are always passed as int& (Tower::purchase, upgrade, sell and CritterGroup::update), so the player's balance lives in one variable owned by the game.

One game tick

The game loop (see playWave in GameDriver.cpp) repeats these steps with a small time step (0.1 s):

  1. every tower gets update(deltaTime, group.getActiveCritters()): cooldown goes down, a target is selected and shot if possible;
  2. group.update(deltaTime, map, coins): spawns the critters whose time has come, moves every critter, adds the reward of the critters killed in step 1 and subtracts the coins stolen by the critters that reached the exit;
  3. the wave is over when group.isFinished() is true. Between waves the player buys, upgrades or sells towers.

Towers shoot first so a critter that is killed is paid in the same tick and is never moved afterwards.

Part 1: Map

  • The grid is a vector<vector<CellType>> that starts as all SCENERY, so a blank map of any size is created with Map(rows, columns) (a size that is not positive throws std::invalid_argument).
  • setCell changes a cell, setEntry and setExit store the coordinates and turn that cell into PATH. All of them return false for coordinates outside the map. The entry and the exit cannot be the same cell and cannot be turned into SCENERY.
  • isValidMap checks that entry and exit are set, inside the map, different, PATH cells, and connected.
  • hasConnectedPath is a breadth-first search from the entry over PATH cells with four-direction moves.
  • Read-only accessors (getRows, getColumns, getEntry, getExit, isPathCell, isSceneryCell) let the other parts use the map without being able to change it. isSceneryCell is what a game uses to refuse a tower placed on the path.
  • Choice: the map does not store towers or critters. It only describes the terrain, so the three parts stay independent.

Part 2: Towers

Tower is an abstract base class. The shared behavior (buying, upgrading, selling, range, cooldown, target selection) lives in the base class and each type only provides its own applyAttack and getName. This is the template method pattern: Tower::update is always the same and calls the virtual applyAttack for the part that changes.

Type Cost Damage Range Shots/s Special
BasicTower 100 20 3.0 1.0 direct damage to one critter
SplashTower 150 30 2.0 0.5 damages every critter within 1.5 cells of the target
SlowTower 125 5 3.0 1.0 small damage and halves the target's speed for 2 s
  • Levels: maximum level 3. Going up one level costs cost x current level, and gives +25% damage (rounded up), +0.5 range and +10% fire rate.
  • Refund: half of all the coins invested (purchase plus upgrades), available at any time with getRefundValue.
  • Targeting: among the alive critters inside the range, the one closest to the tower is selected.
  • Cooldown: after a shot the tower waits 1 / fireRate seconds. A tower that is not bought, or already sold, never shoots.
  • Choice: the target is chosen by distance because it is simple to explain and test. More rules (first, strongest) would fit in selectTarget.

Part 3: Critters

Three small classes with one job each.

Class Responsibility
Critter One critter: stats, shortest path, movement, damage, slow, and one-time claimReward / claimPenalty.
CritterGroup One wave: releases the critters one after the other, updates them, settles the coins.
CritterGroupGenerator Called at the start of a wave, generate(wave, map) builds the group. Refuses wave numbers below 1 and invalid maps.

Critter types** (stats at level 1, taken from the idea of the lane minions of League of Legends):

Type Hit points Strength Speed (cells/s) Reward Critters in a wave
Melee 30 1 1.0 2 3 in every wave
Caster 20 2 1.1 3 3 in every wave
Cannon 60 3 0.8 5 wave / 3 (first one in wave 3)
Super 120 5 0.9 10 wave / 10 (first one in wave 10)

The numbers are our own choices. In the game the cannon minion only appears in some waves, here the extra critters stay in the following waves so that no wave is easier than the one before.

Difficulty:** the level of every critter is the wave number, and from level 1 to level n:

  • hit points grow 20% of the base value per level;
  • strength grows by 1 every 3 levels;
  • speed grows 3% per level, at most +50%;
  • the reward is base reward x level (proportional to the level, as the project description asks);

  • the time between two critters is 0.8 s in wave 1 and 4% shorter every wave (never below 0.32 s), like the tighter streams of later Bloons TD rounds. Because stats only go up and a wave never loses critters, the total hit points of a wave strictly increase (checked for waves 1 to 30).

    Movement:** when a critter spawns it runs a breadth-first search from the entry to the exit (it knows the exit from the map) and keeps the shortest path. Each update adds speed x deltaTime to its progress and moves it to the next cell of the path every time the progress reaches 1, so the speed does not depend on the size of the time step. A critter that reaches the last cell escapes.

    One critter per cell:** the project description says a cell holds only one game element. CritterGroup::update gives every critter a function that tells whether a cell is taken by another active critter. Before a critter steps onto its next cell it asks that function; if the cell is taken it stays at 0.8 of its current cell (right behind the critter in front) and tries again on the next update. Critters never pass each other, so the critters are updated front first: the one in front moves and frees its cell for the one behind. A new critter is released only when the entry is free, so a critter that is due can be delayed a few ticks behind a slower one. The exit is never blocked, because a critter that reaches it leaves the map. A single critter used without a group has no neighbours and walks freely.

    Damage, reward and penalty:**

  • takeDamage lowers the hit points, never below zero, and kills the critter at zero. Only a critter that is on the map can be hit.
  • claimReward returns the reward of a dead critter once, claimPenalty returns the strength of an escaped critter once. CritterGroup::update calls them every tick, so rewards and penalties can never be counted twice.
  • A critter that escapes steals as many coins as its strength, and the player never goes below 0 coins.
  • A slow (applySlow) multiplies the speed by a factor for some seconds. A stronger slow replaces a weaker one.

Other design choices

  • No inheritance for critters: the types only differ in numbers, so one class with a CritterType is enough.
  • Time is passed in seconds to every update, so a console loop, or a GUI later, decides how often things are updated.
  • Critters keep their own copy of the path instead of a pointer to the map, so they cannot be left pointing at a deleted map.
  • Tests and drivers use only the public interface of each class.

Tools and libraries

Standard library only (vector, queue, memory, cmath, algorithm, iostream) for the other drivers Nothing to install for the console build, so the marker can compile it anywhere.
Win32/GDI for the graphical driver No extra GUI library is needed on Windows, the API ships with the MinGW toolchain, the only GUI code lives in Gui/WinGui.cpp, the game classes do not know Win32, and make gui is separate so the rest builds without it.

Own check helper in TestHelper.h A testing framework would be one more thing to install, and the tests are small.
Doxygen The documentation is made from the /// comments and this page.

Testing

Each part has a test program that prints PASS or FAIL for every check and returns a non-zero exit code if one fails (make test).

Program Checks What it covers
map_tests 28 Blank maps and sizes, setting cells, entry and exit rules, valid and invalid maps (no exit, gap in the path, diagonal-only link), saving a map to a file and loading it back, a missing file keeps the old map.
tower_tests 24 Buying, upgrading to the maximum level, refund, selling, range, closest target, cooldown, splash radius, slowing, kills.
critter_tests 60 Stats and reward proportional to the level, damage and death, movement and shortest path with a dead end, slow, wave composition and difficulty from wave 1 to 30, sequential spawning, one critter per cell (waiting behind a blocked cell, queueing behind a slower critter, no spawn on an occupied entry), rewards paid once, coins stolen, coins never below 0.

The drivers show the same behavior on screen: map_driver, tower_driver (buy, upgrade, sell and shoot), critter_driver (generated waves, critters walking, rewards and stolen coins) and game_driver (all parts together over 10 waves).

Graphical driver

The Win32 graphical driver is a thin layer on top of the same rules used by the console drivers. It does not change the game logic.

  • Edit mode: choose Path, Scenery, Entry or Exit, then click or drag on the grid. Rows and columns are chosen separately and can be resized between 3 and 40, so rectangular maps such as 5 x 30 or 10 x 10 are possible. The "Size step" button makes Rows and Cols change by 1, 5 or 10 at a time. Resizing keeps the path cells, the entry and the exit that still fit. The cell size is computed from the map size (the biggest square cell that fits the map area), and the labels, tower letters and level dots are only drawn when they fit in the cell. Blank map and Sample map are shortcuts for the current size or for the 7x7 map used in GameDriver.cpp.
  • Play mode: buy towers on scenery cells, select a tower to see its stats and buttons, start waves, pause or resume the fixed-step simulation, and switch back to the editor when no wave is running.
  • Resizing: the interface is laid out on a fixed 1100 x 720 design area. The window can be resized freely (down to 640 x 440): the whole design area is scaled by one factor (the smaller of width / 1100 and height / 720) with a GDI world transform and centered, so the map, panel, buttons and texts grow and shrink together and nothing is stretched or cut off. Mouse positions are converted back to design coordinates (toDesignPoint), so clicks, hover and hit tests do not depend on the window size.
  • Simulation: time is advanced with a fixed 0.05 s step and an accumulator. Towers shoot first, then critters move and settle rewards and penalties, so the GUI stays consistent with the console driver.

Known limitations

  • The graphical interface is only a thin layer. The only additions to the game classes are the read-only accessors needed for drawing: Critter::getExactPosition, Tower::getUpgradeCost and Tower::getMaxLevel.
  • Critters follow one fixed path, there is no reaction to towers.
  • Bloons TD features such as layered bloons or end-of-round cash are not used.
  • Game balance (costs, damage, rewards) has not been tuned beyond making the waves get harder.
